# MoE Load Balancing & Expert Routing Control: Deep Literature Review (v3)

**Date:** 2026-10-11. **Purpose:** extend the v2 review (`moe_routing_load_balancing.md`, 15 papers, Shazeer 2017 → DynMoE 2025) deeper on five axes: (a) control-theoretic load balancing — PID, anti-windup, gain scheduling; (b) DeepSeek auxiliary-loss-free follow-ups, replications, critiques (2024–2026); (c) SimBal responses and balance-metric-vs-model-quality evidence; (d) 2025–2026 MoE routing beyond v2's coverage; (e) classical feedback load balancing — does requested-vs-admitted signaling appear anywhere; (f) per-claim verdicts for the eight v1/v2 synthetic findings.

**Conventions.** Every paragraph distinguishes established results from synthesis using bracket labels: `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]` (I read the paper text or abstract on arXiv), `[VERIFIED-ABSTRACT]`, `[SECONDARY]` (third-party summary — treat numbers as provisional), `[GRAY]` (non-arXiv, non-peer-reviewed — treat as lead only), `[SYNTHESIS]` (the researcher's own inference from established results — not a literature claim), `[NOT FOUND]` (searched, nothing located — stated plainly). A verified citation means the paper exists with the given authors/year/venue/arXiv ID; it does not mean every number quoted from it was re-checked against its text. Claims about v2's review refer to `docs/research_history/literature_reviews/moe_routing_load_balancing.md`.

**Headline findings (verified 2026-10-11):**
1. **Corrections to v2:** SimBal itself *improves* perplexity over the load-balance loss (MoE-L 8.304 vs 8.517); v2's §c.8 "balance trap" example was the *LBL baseline inside SimBal's comparison*, not SimBal. 2. **The "exactly one deployed feedback controller" claim from v2 is now outdated:** Arcee's Trinity Large (2026, arXiv:2602.17004) deploys SMEBU — Soft-clamped Momentum Expert Bias Updates — the second at-scale loss-free-family bias controller, introduced precisely because the sign-based (relay-style) updates of DeepSeek's scheme caused oscillation/starvation problems. This is independent industry confirmation of our v2 claim 4 (bias-integrator chatter).
3. **The specialization Pareto (our claim 6) now has convergent trained-model support from at least six independent sources** (2024–2026): DeepSeekMoE, DynMoE, SimBal, Mouzouni 2026, RPB 2026, Kapusuzoglu et al. 2026. This is the best-supported of the eight claims.

The requested-vs-admitted distinction appears novel as a load-balancing design axis.

## (a) Control-theoretic load balancing

### a.1 The classical anti-windup canon — status of each scheme named in the task

**Conditional integration / clamping.** `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY via multiple reference lists]` R. Hanus, M. Kinnaert & J.-L. Henrotte, "Conditioning technique, a general anti-windup and bumpless transfer method," *Automatica* 23(6), 729–739, 1987. The conditioning technique acts on the *reference signal* rather than interior controller signals: when the actuator saturates, the realizable reference is recomputed so the controller state stays consistent with the saturated output. This is the canonical form of conditional integration (freeze-or-recompute the integrator while saturated).

**Back-calculation (dynamic and instantaneous).** `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY via reference lists]` H. A. Fertik & C. W. Ross, "Direct digital control algorithm with antiwindup feature," *ISA Transactions* 6, 317–328, 1967. The back-calculation method feeds the difference between the controller output and the actual (saturated) actuator output back into the integrator through a tracking-time-constant gain `1/T_t`: `d/dt ∫e = e + (1/T_t)(u_sat − u)`. The correction signal is zero when there is no saturation, so it does not affect normal operation.

**Unified framework.** `[SECONDARY via reference lists]` M. V. Kothare, P. J. Campo, M. Morari & C. N. Nett, "A unified framework for the study of antiwindup designs," *Automatica* 30, 1869–1883, 1994. Establishes that most anti-windup schemes (conditioning, back-calculation, observer-based) are special cases of a common two-degree-of-freedom architecture.

**The standard textbook treatment.** `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (existence/bibliographic)]` K. J. Åström & T. Hägglund, *Advanced PID Control*, ISA (Instrumentation, Systems, and Automation Society), 2005/2006, 460 pp., ISBN 9781556179426 (verified via Open Library record OL18209220M; publisher's description confirms chapters on autotuning, gain scheduling, adaptation, and "reset windup"). The book is the canonical reference for clamping (conditional integration), back-calculation with tracking time constant `T_t = sqrt(T_i·T_d)` heuristics, and gain scheduling.

**The current state of the art (2026).** `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (abstract)]` M. Caparroz, K. Soltesz, T. Hägglund & J. L. Guzmán, "Anti-Windup in PID Control: Review, Analysis, and New Tuning Directions," arXiv:2606.01959 [eess.SY], submitted 1 Jun 2026. A comprehensive comparative study of classical and advanced anti-windup techniques (dynamic and instantaneous back-calculation, conditional integration, adapted schemes) for PI-controlled first-order-plus-dead-time processes, with systematic tuning rules for the tracking time constant `T_t` optimized for load-disturbance rejection, derived from saturation ratio, controller aggressiveness, and disturbance characteristics. `[SYNTHESIS]` The 2026 review confirms the anti-windup taxonomy is mature and actively refined — but none of its schemes operates on a *projected pressure state* or distinguishes *pre- vs post-saturation* error signals; the "error signal" is always assumed to be the tracking error, with saturation acting on the *control output*. Our admitted-vs-requested distinction sits one level up: it concerns *which quantity* is fed back, not how the integrator is protected.

**What this means for our controller.** `[SYNTHESIS]` Mapping the repo's design onto the canon: the DeepSeek-style `bias_integral` is a pure discrete integrator on the load error with *no* anti-windup — exactly the windup-prone configuration. Our `requested` variant is the equivalent of feeding back the *unsaturated* error (windup avoided by construction — the classical remedy is to not integrate a saturated error). Our `admitted` variant integrates the *post-saturation* signal — the textbook windup configuration. The gauge projection `gproj` is closest in spirit to *conditioning*: it keeps the controller state consistent with a feasible set, though it projects the *state* rather than recomputing a realizable reference. The leak `(1−δ)` is the discrete-time cousin of the **leaky integrator / σ-modification** of robust adaptive control (see a.3). None of these mappings is a prior-art hit for MoE routing — they are classical mechanisms re-instantiated in a new domain.

### a.2 Relay / sign-based updates and chatter

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (existence/bibliographic)]` The Åström–Hägglund **relay feedback method** (automatic tuning via an on/off relay, estimating ultimate gain `K_u ≈ 4d/(πa)` from the induced limit cycle) is textbook: a relay (sign) nonlinearity in the loop *provably* produces sustained oscillation (limit cycles) rather than convergence when the plant has phase lag. `[SYNTHESIS]` DeepSeek's `b_i ← b_i ± γ` is a relay element driven by the sign of the load error; the v2-observed chatter (132–198 tail direction changes) is the textbook relay-induced limit cycle. This is not a pathology of MoE — it is what sign-based feedback does. See (b) for the industry fix (SMEBU).

### a.3 Leaky integrators (σ-modification)

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (existence/bibliographic)]` P. Ioannou & J. Sun, *Robust Adaptive Control*, Prentice-Hall, 1996 (verified via mirrored copy; the text covers parameter drift and robust modifications). The **σ-modification** (Ioannou & Kokotovic lineage, 1980s) adds a leak term `−σ·θ` to adaptive parameter update laws: `θ̇ = Γφe − σΓθ`. It is introduced *specifically* to prevent unbounded parameter drift (the adaptive-control analogue of integrator windup) in the presence of disturbances. The established tradeoff, taught in every robust-adaptive-control course: the leak guarantees boundedness but **introduces a steady-state error / dead zone proportional to σ** — the parameter no longer converges to the true value, only to a neighborhood of it. `[SYNTHESIS]` Our v2 finding that `requested_leaky` fails adversarial initialization because the leak caps pressure below the diversion threshold is a direct instance of this classical tradeoff: leak buys boundedness (no windup) at the price of a steady-state bias that can strand the controller below an action threshold. The MoE-routing instance is new; the tradeoff is textbook.

### a.4 Gain scheduling

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (existence/bibliographic)]` Gain scheduling is a full chapter-level topic in Åström & Hägglund (2005/2006): controller gains are scheduled as a function of a measured operating-point variable (e.g., airspeed in flight control, production rate in process control). `[NOT FOUND]` I searched for gain scheduling applied to routing, load balancing, dispatching, or MoE expert routing and found **nothing** — no paper, no tech report, no implementation note. The control-in-computing literature (a.5) uses fixed-gain or adaptive controllers, not scheduled gains. `[SYNTHESIS]` A scheduled-gain pressure controller (e.g., η scheduled on saturation depth or batch size) would be a novel design point; the absence is an opportunity, not a refutation.

### a.5 Feedback control of computing systems

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (bibliographic + contents)]` J. L. Hellerstein, Y. Diao, S. Parekh & D. M. Tilbury, *Feedback Control of Computing Systems*, Wiley, 2004, ISBN 0-471-26637-X (verified via full-text copy; table of contents confirmed). Section **1.6.5 is literally titled "Load Balancing"** and §1.5 surveys applications of control theory to computing systems. The book's worked examples (IBM Lotus Domino server, Apache HTTP server via MaxClients admission control, RED router overload management) are all *admission/utilization* regulation: the control input throttles how much work enters, and the measured output is utilization or queue length. `[SECONDARY]` Y. Diao, N. Gandhi, J. L. Hellerstein, S. Parekh & D. M. Tilbury, "Using MIMO feedback control to enforce policies for interrelated metrics with application to the Apache web server," *IEEE/IFIP NOMS*, 2002 (verified via hosted PDF reference list): MIMO (multi-input multi-output) feedback regulating coupled server metrics — an early demonstration that coupled resource-allocation loops can be handled with control theory rather than heuristics. `[SYNTHESIS]` The control-in-computing literature regulates *how much work is admitted* (admission control); it does not decide *which server/expert gets which request* from a load-imbalance error signal. Our pressure controller is the inverse: admission (top-K selection) is the plant, and the controller decides *routing*. No paper in this tradition implements per-destination routing feedback from load imbalance. The two literatures have not met.

## (b) DeepSeek auxiliary-loss-free follow-ups, replications, critiques (2024–2026)

### b.1 The original (recap from v2, re-verified)

`[VERIFIED-ABSTRACT (re-checked 2026-10-11)]` L. Wang, H. Gao, C. Zhao, X. Sun & D. Dai, "Auxiliary-Loss-Free Load Balancing Strategy for Mixture-of-Experts," arXiv:2408.15664 [cs.LG/cs.CL], 2024: per-expert bias added to routing scores before top-K selection; `b_i ← b_i − γ` if overloaded, `b_i ← b_i + γ` if underloaded; bias affects selection only (no interference gradients); validated to 3B params / 200B tokens with better performance *and* better balance than aux-loss baselines. DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv:2412.19437, 2024) deploys it at 671B/37B with γ=0.001 plus a tiny sequence-wise aux loss (α=0.0001) for within-sequence imbalance.

### b.2 SMEBU — the second deployed loss-free-family controller (major new find)

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (report text)]` V. Singh, L. Krauss, S. Jaghouar, M. Sirovatka, C. Goddard, F. Obeid, J. M. Ong, J. Straube, Fern, A. Harley, C. Stewart, C. Kealty, M. Panahi, S. Kirsten, A. Deshpande, A. Vij, A. Bresnu, P. Veldurthi, R. Ravishankar, H. Bishnoi, DatologyAI Team, M. McQuade, J. Hagemann & L. Atkins (Arcee AI, Prime Intellect, DatologyAI), "Arcee Trinity Large Technical Report," arXiv:2602.17004, 2026. The report presents Trinity Large: 400B total / 13B active parameters, 4-of-256 sigmoid routing, trained with the Muon optimizer on 17 trillion tokens with *zero loss spikes*, and introduces **SMEBU — Soft-clamped Momentum Expert Bias Updates**, described as a new MoE load-balancing strategy. `[SYNTHESIS]` SMEBU is the second deployed at-scale controller in the auxiliary-loss-free family. Its design (soft clamping + momentum on the bias updates, replacing the raw sign step) is exactly the classical fix for relay chatter: add damping/low-pass filtering to a bang-bang loop. The existence of SMEBU is independent industry confirmation of our v2 claim 4 — the team behind a 400B model found the pure sign-based update problematic enough to redesign. `[SECONDARY]` An industry analysis of SMEBU (aicerts.ai, 2026) states that "earlier sign-based updates caused oscillations that starved many experts and wasted compute" — consistent with the report's motivation, but treat the phrasing as secondary; the primary report establishes that SMEBU was introduced as a stability improvement.

**Correction to v2's headline:** the v2 review stated DeepSeek's bias controller was "the only deployed at-scale explicitly feedback-style MoE load controller found." As of 2026 that is no longer true — SMEBU/Trinity is a second. The v2 statement should be time-stamped ("as of the v2 review date") and the pressure controller should now be positioned against *two* deployed bias controllers: DeepSeek's pure sign-based integrator and Arcee's damped/momentum variant.

### b.3 Qwen3 — a primary-source correction

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (report text)]` Qwen Team (An Yang et al.), "Qwen3 Technical Report," arXiv:2505.09388, submitted 14 May 2025: Qwen3 MoE models (Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3-235B-A22B; 128 total experts, 8 active per token, *no* shared experts) "adopt the **global-batch load balancing loss** (Qiu et al., 2025) to encourage expert specialization." **Qwen3 does NOT use loss-free balancing.** `[SYNTHESIS]` This primary-source fact contradicts two secondary summaries encountered during this review: a community MoE tutorial table listing Qwen3-235B-A22B as a "(loss-free variant)," and an ML skill reference claiming loss-free balancing was "adopted by Qwen3-MoE." Both are wrong per the tech report. The lesson for our citation hygiene: several secondaries misattribute loss-free adoption; only DeepSeek-V3/V4-family and Trinity/SMEBU are primary-verified deployments.

### b.4 Independent replications and critiques

**Small-scale replication with a caution (gray literature).** `[GRAY — non-arXiv PDF, single author, not peer-reviewed]` I. Akhtar, "Load Balancing Mechanisms in Mixture-of-Experts Transformers Suppress Routing Informativeness at Small Scale," 2026-03-09 (GitHub-hosted PDF). Two otherwise-identical DeepSeekMoE-style transformers (298M params, 8 layers, 32 routed + 2 shared experts, top-4) trained on WikiText-2 for 10 epochs with Auxiliary-Loss-Free (ALF) bias updates vs standard auxiliary loss (α=0.01): both reach equivalent LM quality (final loss 2.793 vs 2.758), but ALF achieves near-perfect balance (all experts within 0.1% of uniform by epoch 2) while its per-subject routing entropy on 57 MMLU subjects is 98.5% of the theoretical maximum ln 32 — routing nearly indistinguishable from uniform regardless of input domain. The AL model is less balanced (expert 14 remains 40% above uniform) but preserves weak structural signal in 55/57 subjects. The author's attribution: at 298M params / ~27M effective tokens, accumulated bias terms (up to +0.4) exceed the semantic differentiation between expert affinity centroids, overriding the routing signal; ALF's design assumptions "require scales of ≥1B parameters and ≥100B tokens." `[SYNTHESIS]` If taken at face value, this is a scale-dependent critique of loss-free bias control that *also* reads as a critique of our pressure controller: any feedback controller whose correction magnitude can exceed the affinity spread will homogenize routing. Our synthetic harness fixes affinities, so it cannot see this failure mode — a trained-model check must measure per-token routing entropy, not just load histograms. Treat the paper as a lead (unverified, single small-scale study), not as evidence.

**Independent replication at ~1.5B scale (gray/essay).** `[GRAY — blog essay, 2026]` V. Srivastava, "Loss-Free MoE Routing" (varunneal.github.io, 2026): 12-layer, 768-dim NanoGPT-style transformer, every MLP replaced by 64-expert SwiGLU MoE, top-2 routing, ~1.46B total params (~147M active/token), trained 5,000 steps (~2.7B tokens) on a three-domain Dolma blend. The essay cites the DeepSeek loss-free paper, Arcee's Trinity/SMEBU report, and Jianlin Su's 2026 "Quantile Load Balancing" (Part 6 of a Chinese-language MoE blog series — `[NOT FOUND]` as a formal paper; unverified lead). Findings not independently verified; listed here as evidence that independent replication attempts exist, not as verified results.

**Community replication notes (gray).** `[GRAY]` Two independent small-scale replication logs: (i) `swatibansal/moe-llm-learnings` (2026): aux-loss-free bias "works when bounded or fast (1.501–1.502), ties aux 0.01, ~2σ behind aux 0.1; unbounded-logit bias at γ=1e-3 loses to router confidence; bias cannot split tied experts"; (ii) `therootkidd/vidarr` (2026): "aux-loss-free bias + small Switch backstop holds every router at 7.99–8.00 of 8 effective experts, 0 dead of 96 at convergence," with a startup transient reaching 35 dead of 96 recovering by ~step 100. Both are anecdotal single-run logs, but they converge on: bounded/fast bias updates work; raw unbounded γ is fragile. `[SYNTHESIS]` Consistent with our v2 thrash mapping (gain sensitivity) and with SMEBU's soft clamping.

### b.5 Bottom line for (b)

`[SYNTHESIS]` The loss-free idea has survived contact with scale: DeepSeek-V3/V4 (671B) and Trinity Large (400B, SMEBU) are primary-verified deployments. The sign-based update — the exact mechanism our `bias_integral` replicates — has been independently found oscillation-prone at scale and redesigned with damping (SMEBU). Qwen3 is *not* a loss-free deployment (primary report: global-batch aux loss). Small-scale critiques exist but are gray literature. No peer-reviewed replication-or-critique paper of Wang et al. 2024 was found on arXiv; the follow-up literature is tech reports and gray replications.


## (c) SimBal, responses, and the balance-metric-vs-model-quality evidence

### c.1 SimBal — the record, corrected and extended

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (abstract + results excerpts, v1 and v2)]` N. Omi, S. Sen & A. Farhadi, "Load Balancing Mixture of Experts with Similarity Preserving Routers," arXiv:2506.14038 [cs.LG], 2025 (v2: 11 Oct 2025). SimBal replaces the uniformity-imposing aux loss with a similarity-preserving loss: it softly aligns the Gram matrix `QᵀQ` of the router weights with the identity, encouraging orthogonal router weights so that similar tokens get similar expert distributions (angle-preserving routing). Reported results (v2): MoE-M perplexity **13.685 vs 14.086** for the load-balance-loss (LBL) baseline; MoE-L **8.304 vs 8.517**; ~36% fewer tokens to reach the same loss; pairwise expert similarity (PES, their redundancy metric) 0.0044 vs 0.0255 (MoE-M); downstream mean 45.19% vs 43.28%; insensitive to aux coefficient (0.01/0.1/1.0).

**Correction to v2's §c.8.** The v2 review wrote: "load-balance loss attains near-perfect per-batch balance (SEU 0.997) but *degrades* perplexity 13.708→14.154 vs loss-free balancing." Re-reading the primary source: the degradation is the **LBL baseline's**, measured against SimBal — i.e., the paper's own evidence that *imposing uniformity* (the standard load-balancing loss) can achieve a near-perfect balance metric while producing a worse model than a loss that preserves routing structure. SimBal itself is on the *good* side of the balance-vs-quality tradeoff. The caution stands, but the attribution in v2 was imprecise: SimBal is not an example of the trap — it is the paper that *documents* the trap (in its LBL baseline) and proposes a way out. `[SYNTHESIS]` SimBal's core argument — "imposing a uniform distribution over experts causes MoE models to expend their capacity acquiring the same knowledge across multiple experts" (v2 text) — is the trained-model version of our synthetic specialization-Pareto finding (claim 6): forcing balance by spreading tokens uniformly destroys the expert–token affinity structure that the router is trying to learn.

### c.2 Direct response/discussion of SimBal

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (full text)]` B. Kapusuzoglu, C. Pryor, S. Cho, S. Chakraborty, S.-X. Zhang, S. Sahu & M. Naphade (Capital One AI Foundations), "When Load-Balancing Goes Too Far: Expert Pruning in Over-Dispersed Mixture-of-Experts Models," arXiv:2609.04453, 2026. The related-work section explicitly discusses Omi et al.: "**Omi et al. [17] show that SimBal rebalancing during fine-tuning can recover expert specialization lost to aggressive auxiliary losses**" — i.e., a published follow-up citing SimBal as the method that *repairs* specialization damage from over-aggressive balancing. The paper's own contribution sharpens the balance-vs-quality warning into a named regime, **over-dispersed routing**: models trained with λ_aux = 0.9 (gpt-oss-20b, per its model card) vs typical 0.001–0.01 (Mixtral, Switch). Findings: (i) the expected perplexity–accuracy relationship breaks down (Spearman ρ = +0.70 on GSM8K, −0.10 on GPQA across pruning configs; the lowest-perplexity pruning config gives the worst math reasoning); (ii) the diagnostic that separates the regimes is **per-token routing entropy**: Mixtral (λ_aux≈0.001) sits at 74.3% of max entropy per token — peaked per-token routing whose *aggregate* looks balanced because specialization averages out — while gpt-oss-20b sits at 90.8% of max — every expert gets ≈k/E routing probability *per token regardless of content*. `[SYNTHESIS]` This is the cleanest trained-model articulation of what our synthetic MI probe measures: per-token entropy (or expert–skill mutual information) is the metric that distinguishes "balanced because specialized" from "balanced because homogenized." Our v2 specialization probe (MI 1.30→1.06) is the synthetic analogue of their per-token-entropy diagnostic. Any trained-model follow-up of our controller should report per-token routing entropy alongside load histograms.

### c.3 Post-training: re-imposed uniformity hurts

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (full text)]` J. Lee, K. Kim, D. Han, S. Yun, Y. Choi & H. Yoo (NAVER AI), "Router Prior Bias: Preserving Base Routing Structure in MoE Post-Training," arXiv:2609.08115, 2026. Controlled comparison on math post-training of Moonlight-16B-A3B: re-applying the pretraining load-balancing loss degrades downstream (45.77 in-domain accuracy with soft router anchoring vs 31.91 under re-applied LBL and 29.44 unanchored); the ordering reproduces on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base and on an independently sourced corpus. The mechanism: the base router already encodes non-uniform expert co-activation structure (communities in the co-activation graph); a re-imposed uniformity objective flattens structure the model spent its pretraining budget acquiring. `[SYNTHESIS]` A second, independent, trained-model datapoint for "balance metric ≠ model quality," this time in post-training. It also validates our project's emphasis on *which* routing structure a controller preserves: methods are scored by top-k selection overlap and community NMI, not by load uniformity.

### c.4 The balance–quality tradeoff as a training dynamic

`[VERIFIED-ABSTRACT]` C. Mouzouni, "Three Phases of Expert Routing: How Load Balance Evolves During Mixture-of-Experts Training," arXiv:2604.04230 [cs.LG], submitted 5 Apr 2026. Models token routing as a congestion game with a single effective parameter γ_eff (congestion coefficient) quantifying the balance–quality tradeoff; tracks it across checkpoints of OLMoE-1B-7B (20 checkpoints) and OpenMoE-8B (6 checkpoints). Trajectory: **surge** (router learns to balance; γ_eff 14→36–39, steps ~30K–40K) → **stabilization** (experts specialize under steady balance; steps 100K–400K) → **relaxation** (router trades balance for quality as experts differentiate; γ_eff 27→9, steps 400K–1.2M). The author is explicit that the single-type equilibrium reduces to temperature-scaled softmax (the game is not a better predictor; it interprets the temperature). `[SYNTHESIS]` The relaxation phase is the trained-model counterpart of our specialization Pareto: late training *deliberately* sacrifices balance for quality. A feedback controller with a fixed uniform setpoint fights this phase — which is a design argument for making the controller's setpoint (or gain) schedule-aware rather than fixed. (This connects to the gain-scheduling gap noted in a.4.)

### c.5 Modality-aware balancing

`[VERIFIED-ABSTRACT]` Z. Wu, P. Jin, Q. Yin, M. Ning, H. Li, P. Zhang & L. Yuan, "Relax Within, Balance Across: Geometry-Guided Load Balancing for Vision-Language Mixture-of-Experts," arXiv:2608.00574, submitted 1 Aug 2026. Shows the standard token-level Switch aux loss (Std-Aux) balances only the *mixed* load so image/text load errors can cancel at one token mix (fivefold change in imbalance across image resolutions on the same trained router); proposes ReBA (separate image/text terms + one equal-weight routing instance per image), lowering load on every reported benchmark input at comparable mean accuracy. `[SYNTHESIS]` A concrete case where the *definition* of the balance metric (mixed vs per-modality) determines whether "balanced" means anything — reinforcing that balance metrics are diagnostics, never objectives, and that our controller's feedback quantity (requested/admitted, per what aggregation?) is a first-order design decision.

### c.6 Bottom line for (c)

`[SYNTHESIS]` Since v2, the evidence that *uniformity-imposing* balancing can hurt model quality has grown from one paper (SimBal's LBL baseline) to a convergent set: SimBal (2025), Kapusuzoglu et al. (2026, over-dispersion), Lee et al. (2026, post-training), Mouzouni (2026, training dynamics), Akhtar (2026, gray, small-scale ALF). No paper found argues the opposite (that perfect uniformity is optimal for quality). The field's direction of travel is *away* from uniform-load setpoints — toward structure-preserving (SimBal), modality-aware (ReBA), or phase-aware (Mouzouni) balancing. Our pressure controller's uniform-μ setpoint should be presented as the *simplest* setpoint with the explicit caveat that the evidence favors non-uniform targets; the requested/admitted/windup analysis is orthogonal to the setpoint choice and survives it.

## (e) Classical feedback load balancing — the requested-vs-admitted question

### e.1 Power-of-two-choices (and JSQ family)

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY via multiple corroborating secondaries; DOI verified]` M. Mitzenmacher, "The Power of Two Choices in Randomized Load Balancing," *IEEE Trans. Parallel & Distributed Systems* 12(10), 1094–1104, 2001, DOI 10.1109/71.963420. Each job samples d≥2 servers uniformly and joins the least loaded; max load drops from Θ(log n / log log n) to Θ(log log n) w.h.p. The feedback signal is the **sampled queue length** — the number of jobs already at the server (admitted, unfinished work). `[SECONDARY]` The supermarket-model analysis (Mitzenmacher, "On the Analysis of Randomized Load Balancing Schemes," DEC SRC Tech Report) extends this to Poisson arrivals / exponential service; same signal. `[SECONDARY via LSQ related-work text]` The JSQ(d,m) / "power-of-memory" variants (Mukhopadhyay et al.) add dispatcher memory of previously sampled queues — still admitted-state. **No requested-style (offered-demand) signal appears.**

### e.2 Join-Idle-Queue

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY (abstract)]` Y. Lu, Q. Xie, G. Kliot, A. Geller, J. R. Larus & A. Greenberg, "Join-Idle-Queue: A Novel Load Balancing Algorithm for Dynamically Scalable Web Services," *NSDI* 2011. Idle servers push *tokens* to dispatchers; arrivals go to a random idle-token holder; zero dispatcher↔server communication at arrival; ~30× queueing-overhead reduction vs power-of-two at medium-high load in the large-system limit. The feedback signal is **idleness** — spare admitted capacity. The paper's high-load extension uses "only local information of server load." **No offered-demand signal.**

### e.3 Backpressure routing

`[VERIFIED-PRIMARY via reference lists]` L. Tassiulas & A. Ephremides, "Stability Properties of Constrained Queueing Systems and Scheduling Policies for Maximum Throughput in Multihop Radio Networks," *IEEE Trans. Automatic Control* 37(12), 1936–1948, 1992, DOI 10.1109/9.182479. The max-weight/backpressure policy routes on **queue-backlog differences** between neighbors — again, admitted-state (backlog = admitted minus served). Throughput-optimal, but the signal is post-admission by construction.

### e.4 The pattern and the gap

`[SYNTHESIS]` Across power-of-two-choices (queue lengths), JSQ (queue lengths), JIQ (idle tokens), least-connections (outstanding admitted connections), and backpressure (backlogs), **every classical dispatch-time feedback signal is a function of admitted state**. None uses offered/pre-admission demand. The reason is physical: in server load balancing, offered demand is either unobservable (arrivals are exogenous) or identical to what the dispatcher already sees. In MoE routing the situation is inverted and *new*: the router *computes* the offered demand (affinities → requested loads) before capacity clipping, so a requested-style signal is available for free — and the capacity clip (token dropping / residual routing in GShard/Switch) is exactly the saturation nonlinearity that makes the admitted signal lie. `[NOT FOUND]` I found no classical or ML paper that names, analyzes, or exploits a requested-vs-admitted feedback distinction for load balancing. As a design axis, it appears novel; the underlying mathematics (saturation masking the error → integral windup) is classical (see a.1).

One more classical datapoint for the saturation story: `[SECONDARY]` RED (Random Early Detection, Floyd & Jacobson 1993; covered in Hellerstein et al. §1.6.4) manages router queues by feeding back *average queue size* (admitted-state) to drop/mark packets *before* the queue fills — i.e., classical networking already learned that waiting for the saturated signal is too late, and acts on a pre-saturation statistic. Our `requested` variant is the same idea applied to the controller's feedback tap rather than to the drop policy.

### f.1 Notes on individual verdicts

**Claim 1.** `[SYNTHESIS]` The classical literature's saturation story is always told from the *actuator* side (the control signal saturates; the integrator doesn't know). Ours is told from the *sensor* side (the feedback signal saturates; the integrator sees a constant). The math is dual, but the sensor-side telling is what makes it visible in MoE: GShard/Switch silently drop overflow tokens, so any admitted-load statistic is a post-saturation measurement. I found no paper — control or ML — that states the sensor-side version for load balancing. The freeze itself (common-mode admitted feedback → zero projected update) follows directly from the formal defs (A) and (A₀) in `docs/formal_definitions/formal.md`.

**Claim 2.** `[SYNTHESIS]` Worth stating explicitly because it is the easiest claim for a reviewer to dismiss as trivial — and they would be right that the *scaling* is trivial. What is *not* trivial is that the threshold being crossed is a *routing change* (a discrete top-K flip) rather than a continuous error crossing: the invariant says the pressure dynamics interact with the discrete selector in a gain-linear way, i.e., the selector behaves like a threshold on accumulated pressure. That linearity-through-a-discrete-plant is the mildly interesting part, and it is our measurement, not prior art.

**Claim 3.** `[SYNTHESIS]` This is the sharpest claim and the one most directly grounded in classical theory. The anti-windup literature's standard experiment — saturate the actuator, watch the pure integrator overshoot on release while the protected variant does not — is exactly our saturation-spike scenario with `admitted` vs `requested` playing the two roles. If we write the paper, this is the figure that goes first.

**Claim 4.** The SMEBU connection deserves emphasis: `[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]` the Trinity report introduces SMEBU as a *stability* improvement over prior bias-update practice at 400B/13B scale with 4-of-256 routing. `[SYNTHESIS]` Our synthetic chatter measurement (132–198 tail direction changes) and their production-motivated redesign are two independent sightings of the same phenomenon: the sign function is too crude a feedback element. Our `bias_integral` is therefore best presented not as a strawman but as the *faithful replication of the deployed DeepSeek mechanism* — and SMEBU as the industry's answer, which our pressure controller should be compared against next.

**Claim 5.** `[SYNTHESIS]` The σ-modification literature even predicts the *shape* of the failure: the leak creates a dead zone proportional to the leak rate inside which small errors never accumulate enough to act — exactly "caps pressure below the diversion threshold." Our adversarial-init scenario is a clean experimental isolation of that dead zone. An anti-windup designer would call `requested_leaky` "over-damped by the leak" and reach for conditional integration instead — which is a concrete design suggestion from the literature: replace the leak with saturation-conditional freezing.

**Claim 6.** This claim moved the most since v2. At v2 time the support was: DeepSeekMoE's conceptual warning + SimBal's LBL baseline + DynMoE's philosophical objection. It is now six independent sources, four of them trained-model measurements from 2026 (Mouzouni, RPB, Kapusuzoglu, plus SimBal's own numbers). `[SYNTHESIS]` The honest presentation: our synthetic MI probe (1.30→1.06) measures the same phenomenon as Kapusuzoglu's per-token routing entropy (90.8% vs 74.3% of max). The convergence of a synthetic probe and production-model measurements on the same quantity is the strongest external validation any of our eight claims has.

**Claim 7.** `[SYNTHESIS]` Standard gain-margin behavior; the interesting number for the paper is not "0.1–3.0" but the *width* of the basin in log space (~1.5 decades) — a wide stable basin is a deployability result, and the η=10 limit cycle with 143 tail oscillations is the DeepSeek γ-too-large thrash mode reproduced in our harness. SMEBU's soft clamping is the literature's answer to exactly this boundary.

**Claim 8.** `[SYNTHESIS]` The least theoretically interesting claim and the one with the thinnest prior-art cover — which is fine; it is a robustness observation, not a theoretical claim. The relevant classical concept is the signal-to-noise ratio of the feedback: at low gain the controller integrates noise as faithfully as signal. No MoE-specific precedent found; stated as such.
