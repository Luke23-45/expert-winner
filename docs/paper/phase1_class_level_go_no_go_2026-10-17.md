# Class-level Top-K theorem: one go/no-go attempt

**Date:** 2026-10-17

**Branch:** `feat/research-censored-feedback`
**Decision:** **NO-GO for a new control-limitation claim on the present theorem and benchmark.** Preserve the proved family lemma as a scoped MoE structural result. Do not infer that all future Top-K theory is impossible.

## 1. Question fixed before the assessment

The family is the canonical static E-world Top-1 construction in
[`phase1_eworld_pair_separation_gate_2026-10-16.md`](phase1_eworld_pair_separation_gate_2026-10-16.md):
`N` regular experts plus one sink; one size-`C` baseline block per regular
expert; world `i` raises the margin of its own block and adds `E_S` extra
tokens; uniform prior; capacities `C` on regular experts and `C_sink` on the
sink; deterministic tie-breaking; gauge-quotient pressure. Assume
`E_S ≤ C_sink < C − E_S/2`, and write `L=E_S+C−C_sink`.

For the comparison, use the independently stated B1 benchmark in
[`phase1_benchmark_gate_2026-10-16.md`](phase1_benchmark_gate_2026-10-16.md):
finite hypotheses and prior, metric action space with movement radius,
piecewise-constant outcomes on a finite affine-hyperplane arrangement,
fixed finite output alphabet/loss, and the requested/admitted comparison
with clipping and mass conservation. The benchmark retains the metric and
movement constraint but does not impose Top-K token-consistency on outcome
labels.

The family-level candidate conclusion is the pair-separation inequality
\[
\mathbb E[J(q,W)\mid W\in U]\;\ge\;\frac L2\,
\Delta_q(U),\qquad
\Delta_q(U)=|U|-\frac1{|U|}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P_q(U)}|B|^2,
\]
for every nonempty posterior support `U` and every pressure `q`.

## 2. What the existing proof actually buys

The result is parameterized in the number of worlds/regular experts. Its
mechanism is Top-1-specific: all token types share a common hub, and every
regular expert has one of three low/high destination patterns. The resulting
request and overflow formulas imply that the cost of each world is at least
`L` times its contribution to the number of separated unordered pairs.
Summing over the support and using
`Δ=2 × (separated unordered pairs)/|U|` proves the bound.

The statement is stronger than the finite E=4 audit: the audit checks the
finite arrangement signatures and supports; the argument above proves the
inequality for the stated canonical family and all pressures. It is not a
general theorem for arbitrary Top-K routers, arbitrary affinities, or
heterogeneous capacities. It is also a one-step information-cost inequality,
not by itself a finite-horizon value gap. The unrestricted-movement Bellman
recursion is exact for this family, but it remains a bounded-depth
outcome-dependent decision-tree problem.

## 3. Reduction attack

### Exact instance simulation

The B1 construction in the benchmark note represents each finite Top-K
instance by the same pressure space and metric, with hyperplanes at all
pairwise score ties. Each arrangement face receives the exact request,
admitted, and overflow outputs. This preserves every policy tree and value.
Consequently, no fixed-instance strict value separation from B1 is possible.

### Does the pair-separation inequality transfer as a universal B1 guarantee?

No. B1 allows piecewise-constant labels that are not generated by
token-consistent Top-1 scores. The benchmark note's mass-conserving
counterexample starts with aliased admitted observations and then uses a
reachable action whose requests are capacity-feasible, have zero overflow,
and identify the world. On a support with at least two worlds this gives
`Δ>0` and `E[J]=0`, violating any positive lower bound of the displayed
form. A finite affine arrangement can separate the initial action from the
identifying action, so this is admissible in B1.

This establishes a **class restriction**: the canonical Top-1 family has a
positive information-cost guarantee that the unrestricted B1 class does not
have uniformly. It does not establish an optimal-value advantage over B1 for
the same instance—B1 exactly simulates that instance—or a new decision-tree
principle. Once B1 is restricted to the same family/outcome map, the lemma
transfers unchanged because it is simply the same experiment.

### Prior-art reduction risk

The mathematical shape—relating the cost of a test to pair separation—is
already used in decision-tree optimization. Outcome-dependent testing costs
are also an established model, and movement-constrained active hypothesis
testing predates this work. The primary-source checks are summarized in the
research-status ledger and cited in the E-world note. Those papers do not
prove this exact Top-1 inequality, but the current result does not yet show a
new general principle that survives the exact experiment representation.

## 4. Go/no-go result

| Gate | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a parameterized theorem rather than only a finite audit? | Yes: PS holds across the stated canonical E-world Top-1 family. | Preserve it as a proved structural lemma. |
| Is its mechanism tied to Top-K/root geometry? | Yes, through the common-hub/three-class winner structure. | It is legitimately MoE-specific under the declared family assumptions. |
| Does it create a fixed-instance value separation from B1? | No; B1 exactly simulates each finite instance. | Fixed-instance novelty route is closed. |
| Does the comparison establish a useful class-level value/complexity advantage over an independently fixed benchmark? | No. The current result is a one-step inequality; broad B1 defeats a universal version, while narrowing B1 to the same family makes the result transfer verbatim. | The comparison is not yet a conference-level separation. |
| Does the checked literature establish this exact lemma as known? | Not shown. The reviewed work establishes close pair-separation and test-cost methods, not this exact inequality. | Do not call it predated; do not call it novel/exhaustive either. |

**Decision:** no-go for the paper claim “a new general limitation of
admitted-feedback control” based on current Phase-1 work. The defensible
result is an exact MoE application plus a candidate structural lemma for a
canonical Top-1 family. A future change in this decision requires a proved
family-level finite-horizon value or complexity theorem whose quantitative
dependence on Top-K/root-arrangement constraints cannot be obtained merely
by encoding the same outcome maps in B1, and a prior-art audit at that exact
theorem boundary.

This is one completed go/no-go attempt, not authorization for an unbounded
sequence of “next phases.” No further fixed-instance enumeration, grid
refinement, or experiment sweep is recommended to address this theorem gate.

## 6. Follow-up audit of the two-step overflow theorem

The later theorem in
[`heterogeneous_capacity_two_step_overflow_theorem_2026-10-17.md`](heterogeneous_capacity_two_step_overflow_theorem_2026-10-17.md)
is a new cost consequence inside the canonical proportional-pressure model:
for its stated iid Top-1 family, admitted feedback has a strictly larger
asymptotic two-step expected overflow than requested feedback. It does not
reverse this go/no-go decision.

For every fixed finite affinity matrix, the theorem's two-step trajectory is
exactly represented by B1 (or B1-out) using the finite Top-1 tie arrangement:
the first-step count vector has finitely many values, so each controller's
second-step pressure is one of finitely many count-determined pressures; the
arrangement at those pressures fixes both step outcomes. Therefore the
paired overflow difference for that realized matrix is exactly simulable by
the benchmark. The controlled rational-grid experiments use a finite prior
over matrices, so the entire experiment is an exact finite B1-out instance.

The theorem itself uses continuous affinity draws rather than a finite prior.
For each fixed $T$, however, its bounded two-step loss as a function of the
affinity matrix is constant off a finite union of score-tie surfaces in
affinity space: only the initial pressure and the finitely many
count-determined next pressures can be queried. These surfaces have
Lebesgue measure zero under the continuous iid law. Consequently, uniform
rational-grid priors approximate its finite-$T$ expected gap arbitrarily
closely, and each such grid experiment embeds exactly in B1-out. This rules
out presenting the positive formula itself as a value separation from the
general outcome-map benchmark. It remains a mathematically concrete
Top-1/capacity consequence; a conference-level theory claim still needs a
quantitative property that does not transfer under this reduction.

The paired experiments and full finite-size deviations are documented in
[`heterogeneous_capacity_two_step_results_2026-10-17.md`](heterogeneous_capacity_two_step_results_2026-10-17.md)
and [`heterogeneous_capacity_family_results_2026-10-17.md`](heterogeneous_capacity_family_results_2026-10-17.md).

### Follow-up: arbitrary `K` in the two-step family theorem

The later Top-K extension proves, for every fixed `E>=2` and `1<=K<E`, an
asymptotic expected two-step gap `Phi_(E,K)(d)-K/E>0` under its stated iid
uniform-affinity and exact-total-capacity assumptions. Its controlled run
covers every `K` for `E={3,4,8}`. This extends the Top-1 formula; it does not
alter the benchmark conclusion.

For fixed `T`, the initial Top-K count vector ranges over a finite set, so
the requested and admitted updates query only finitely many second-step
pressures. The two-step outcomes as functions of the affinity matrix are
constant away from the corresponding finite union of Top-K tie surfaces.
Thus the same finite-rational-grid approximation and B1-out encoding apply:
each finite grid prior is represented exactly, and continuous-affinity
finite-`T` expected costs are limits of such finite priors. Consequently this
all-`K` family result is not a strict value separation from the unrestricted
polyhedral outcome-map benchmark. It strengthens the existing MoE application
but does not reopen the class-level novelty gate.

### Follow-up: joint facet/overflow result

The joint result in
[`joint_topk_overflow_facet_theorem_2026-10-17.md`](joint_topk_overflow_facet_theorem_2026-10-17.md)
strengthens the specified Top-K two-step gap to convergence in probability
and proves that its positive-gap event and the unconditioned regular-facet
event co-occur on the same iid affinity matrix with probability tending to
one. The registered finite-grid experiment found both events on all 150
plants, and its 450 overflow rows exactly match the preceding E=4,K=2 run.

This still does not reverse the B1/B1-out conclusion. For a finite prior
over affinity matrices, construct one common arrangement from all
world/token/expert-pair score-tie hyperplanes
\[
 a^w_{t,e}-a^w_{t,f}-\gamma(q_e-q_f)=0.
\]
Refine the arrangement by all worlds' hyperplanes. On every relatively open
cell and face, label each world/action with the exact tie-broken Top-K
request vector `R^w(q)`, its clipped vector `A^w(q)=min(R^w(q),C)`, and its
overflow vector `J^w(q)=R^w(q)-A^w(q)`. This is an admissible finite
piecewise-constant outcome map for B1-out. It preserves the pressure metric,
movement constraint, observation histories, and pathwise costs. The same
labels on adjacent cells preserve every admitted-field facet jump as well.
Thus this benchmark copy reproduces both the overflow trajectories and the
facet event exactly for every finite-grid plant; the finite-grid joint audit
has no strict instance-level value or geometry separation from B1-out.

The continuous-law theorem is a property of the specified Top-K
random-affinity family, while unrestricted B1-out can represent every finite
instance in that sequence. Co-occurrence also does not establish that
non-potentiality causes the overflow gap. The new result is a sound
same-model synthesis, not the missing Top-K-specific benchmark-separating
contribution.

The targeted primary-source check of the nearest potential-based MoE method,
[φ-Balancing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15403), confirms that it optimizes
a potential of the population routing-probability distribution through
mirror descent; it does not analyze the post-capacity field
`A_e=min(R_e,C_e)`. This is an equation-level distinction, not novelty
clearance. The joint protocol, paired data, runner, and hashes are recorded
in [`joint_facet_overflow_results_2026-10-17.md`](joint_facet_overflow_results_2026-10-17.md).
