# Two-step overflow harm from admitted pressure under heterogeneous capacities

**Research line:** the canonical Top-$K$, per-expert clipping, requested-vs-admitted pressure model in `formal.md`.
**Evidence:** asymptotic theorem for a fixed iid-affinity family plus a preregistered paired exact-grid experiment.
**Question:** does the admitted field's loss of request information have a direct finite-horizon system-cost consequence under the original pressure update?

## Theorem

Let $E=3$, $K=1$, $\gamma=1$, $\mu=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, zero leak, and initial
pressure $q_0=0$. Fix rational $r,\sigma$ with $0<r<1/3$ and
$0\le\sigma\le r$.
For each $T$ along an integer sequence making the capacities integral, draw
one static affinity matrix with iid entries $a_{t,e}\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]$
and use it at both routing steps. Set exact-total heterogeneous capacities
\[
C(T)=T(1/3-r,\;1/3+\sigma,\;1/3+r-\sigma),\qquad \sum_e C_e=T=KT.
\]
At step $s$, route each token to its highest score $a_{t,e}-q_e$, compute
$R_s$, $A_s=\min(R_s,C)$, and overflow
\[
J_s=\sum_e(R_{s,e}-A_{s,e}).
\]
For signal $L\in\{R,A\}$, update by the canonical gauge pressure rule
\[
q_{s+1}^{L}=\Pi_g\!\left(q_s^L+\eta\left(\frac{L_s}{T}-\mu\right)\right),
\qquad s=0.
\]
Both arms use the same sampled affinity matrix and the same initial route.

For every fixed $\eta>0$ such that $d=\eta r<1$,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{T\to\infty,\;C(T)\in\mathbb{N}^3}
\frac{1}{T}\,\mathbb E\!\left[(J_0^A+J_1^A)-(J_0^R+J_1^R)\right]
=d-\frac{d^3}{3}>0.}
\]
The limit is independent of how the excess capacity $rT$ is split between
experts 2 and 3, provided both have at least $T/3$. The first-step overflow
is identical in the two arms; the entire limiting gap is generated by the
second routing step. The no-feedback arm has the same limiting cost as the
requested arm over these two steps. The earlier capacity vector
$(1/6,1/2,1/3)$ is the case $r=\sigma=1/6$, for which $0<\eta<6$.

## Proof

At $q_0=0$, exchangeability and the law of large numbers give
$R_0/T\to(1/3,1/3,1/3)$ almost surely. Clipping at the capacity fractions
therefore gives
\[
A_0/T\to(1/3-r,1/3,1/3).
\]
The requested update converges to $q_1^R=0$. The admitted update has
uncentered increment $\eta(-r,0,0)$; subtracting its minimum gives
\[
q_1^A\longrightarrow(0,d,d),\qquad d=\eta r.
\]

At requested pressure $q=0$, the population route probabilities remain
$(1/3,1/3,1/3)$. At admitted pressure $(0,d,d)$, the three score variables
are $U_0,U_1-d,U_2-d$ for iid $U_e\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]$. For
$0<d<1$, the probability that expert 0 wins is
\[
p_0(d)=\int_0^1\min\{1,x+d\}^2\,dx
=\frac13+d-\frac{d^3}{3}.
\]
Symmetry gives $p_1(d)=p_2(d)=(1-p_0(d))/2<1/3$. Since capacities 2 and 3
are each at least $1/3$, only the under-capacity expert (expert 0) overflows
under the admitted limit;
its overflow fraction is $p_0(d)-(1/3-r)=r+p_0(d)-1/3$. Under requested or
no feedback, the second-step overflow fraction is $r$. Their difference is
$p_0(d)-1/3=d-d^3/3$.

Although the same sampled affinity matrix is used at both steps, the
empirical Top-1 route fractions at the random pressures $q_1^L$ converge to
the population probabilities at their deterministic limits. First,
$q_1^L$ converges almost surely by the first-step law of large numbers and
continuity of coordinatewise clipping and gauge projection. Second, the
empirical winner proportions converge uniformly over pressure in a compact
neighborhood of the limiting pressure: the winner indicators form a
finite-VC class, since each winner region is an intersection of two
affine score-comparison half-spaces in the pressure parameter. The population
winner probabilities are continuous at the limiting pressure because
independent continuous affinities give zero probability to a score tie.
Uniform convergence plus this continuity handles the dependence of $q_1^L$
on the same affinity sample used at step one. Thus the normalized paired
overflow gap converges almost surely to the displayed constant. It is
uniformly bounded, so its expectation converges to that constant as well.
Since step-zero overflow is identical, this proves the displayed two-step
limit. $\square$

## Interpretation

This is a cost consequence of the exact clipping map and the canonical
pressure update, not a replacement model. At the initial state, the
capacity-limited first expert is admitted below the uniform requested
target. Admitted feedback therefore reduces its relative pressure, making it
more attractive on the next routing pass. Requested feedback sees the
balanced request proportions and produces a vanishing pressure update.
The theorem gives an $\Theta(T)$ cumulative-overflow difference for each
fixed rational capacity pair $(r,\sigma)$ and gain $\eta>0$ satisfying
$\eta r<1$ in this declared random-affinity family.

The result is not a universal ranking: it is a specific exact-total
heterogeneous-capacity family and a two-step horizon. It does not establish
a trained-model quality gain, a physical telemetry price, or a value gap
against the unrestricted polyhedral outcome-map benchmark, which can encode
each finite instance exactly. It adds the missing route-transition-to-cost
link for one parameterized family; a top-conference claim still requires a
broader family theorem or a benchmark distinction that survives the known
reduction.

The theorem does not claim the balancing controller itself as new. Loss-Free
Balancing already updates an expert bias using the sign of the load error
([primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664)); ID Balancing explicitly
places that update in an integral-control framework and adds magnitude-aware
integral and gated-derivative terms
([primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39137)). Those papers make
generic claims about feedback or PID novelty unavailable. The present
statement concerns the cost consequence of the separately specified
proportional gauge-pressure update in `formal.md` under the stated clipping
family. This boundary is not novelty clearance.
