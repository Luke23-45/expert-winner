# Controlled protocol: exact arbitrary-E Top-1 swapped-value family

## Question

Check the predicted signal behavior and finite-batch overflow consequences
for the exact `v*=1/36` Top-1 family in
[`top1_arbitrary_e_exact_swapped_value_theorem_2026-10-17.md`](top1_arbitrary_e_exact_swapped_value_theorem_2026-10-17.md).
This is a synthetic theorem-consistency experiment, not a model-training or
hardware-cost study.

## Locked design

- Expert counts: `E in {3,4,8,16,32,64}`; `K=1`.
- World-0 token probabilities: `(5/13,4/13,4/(13(E-2)),...,4/(13(E-2)))`.
- World 1 swaps the first two token probabilities. Paired world-1 count
  vectors are generated by swapping the first two entries of each world-0
  multinomial draw, an exact coupling.
- Capacities: `C_1=C_2=T/4`, `C_j=T/(2(E-2))` for `j>=3`. Use
  `T=4(E-2)*s`, `s in {1,4,16}`, so every capacity is integer and
  `sum C_e=T` exactly.
- Replications: 2,000 paired first-batch draws per `(E,T)` cell, with a
  fixed published seed schedule.
- Signals: full requested counts `R` and clipped admitted counts
  `A=min(R,C)`.
- Registered policies: sign classifier on `R_1-R_2` or `A_1-A_2`; exact
  ties are assigned expected error `1/2` and the mean of the two action
  risks. These classifiers are diagnostics, not claimed optimal policies.
- The next-batch population actions are the exact target pressure for each
  world, `rho=c`. For each first-batch classifier outcome, calculate the
  next-batch expected normalized overflow from exact binomial marginals;
  do not draw a second batch.
- Record paired admitted-vector equality, paired unclipped-request
  equality, classifier errors, expected overflow, Monte Carlo standard
  errors, integer mass/capacity checks, and exact world-swap checks.

## Controls and interpretation

The unclipped equality indicator is the no-censoring control. At every cell,
check that request mass is `T`, capacity mass is `T`, and world 1 is exactly
the coordinate swap of world 0. The analytic value `1/36` is a population
common-action optimum; the finite-sample classifier outcomes do not estimate
that optimum. No hardware latency, all-reduce surcharge, training quality,
or deployment prevalence is inferred.
