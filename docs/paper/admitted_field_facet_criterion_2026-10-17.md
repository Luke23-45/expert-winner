# Facet criterion for convexity of admitted Top-K feedback

**Evidence class:** analytic characterization on regular Top-K arrangement
facets, plus an exact-rational local obstruction audit.

## Setup

Fix a static batch of $T$ affinity vectors, $E$ experts, Top-$K$ routing with
$K<E$, positive integer per-expert capacities $C$, and deterministic routing
away from score ties. Let $R(q)$ be the requested counts,
$A(q)=\min(R(q),C)$ coordinatewise, and define the centered admitted field
$$
g_A(q)=P_0(KT\mu-A(q))
$$
on the pressure quotient $\mathbb R^E/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$.
The requested field $P_0(KT\mu-R(q))$ is already the gradient of the
established convex hard Top-K potential, up to positive scaling.

Consider a regular codimension-one routing facet. Every route exchange active
on its relative interior uses the same expert pair $i,j$: distinct pairwise
Top-$K$ tie normals are not parallel and cannot define the same
codimension-one hyperplane. A facet may carry simultaneous exchanges for
several tokens when their $i,j$ tie hyperplanes coincide. Orient the facet so
these exchanges move from $i$ to $j$ from the minus side to the plus side,
and let $m$ be their number.

## Theorem: local facet test and global cell-interior criterion

Let $R^-$ and $R^+$ be the count vectors on the two sides, so
$R^+=R^- -m e_i+m e_j$. Define the aggregate admitted source decrease and
destination increase
$$
d_i=\min(R_i^-,C_i)-\min(R_i^--m,C_i),\qquad
d_j=\min(R_j^-+m,C_j)-\min(R_j^-,C_j).
$$
Then
$$
A^+-A^-=-d_i e_i+d_j e_j,
\qquad
g_A^+-g_A^-=P_0(d_i e_i-d_j e_j).
$$
For a single-token facet ($m=1$), this reduces to
$d_i=\mathbf1\{R_i^-\le C_i\}$ and
$d_j=\mathbf1\{R_j^-<C_j\}$.

For $E\ge3$:

1. If $d_i\ne d_j$ at any regular facet, the admitted field on the
   adjacent open cells cannot be generated by a continuous potential that is
   affine on routing cells; in particular, the field cannot be a subgradient
   selection of any convex potential on the full pressure quotient.
2. If $d_i=d_j$ at every regular facet, the field on open cells is the
   gradient of a convex piecewise-affine potential. In the matched cases the
   gradient jump is $d_iP_0(e_i-e_j)$, with $d_i\ge0$; this includes zero,
   partial, and full admitted-count changes.

Thus, for a regular Top-$K$ arrangement and $E\ge3$, a convex-potential
representation on all open routing cells exists exactly when each regular
facet has equal aggregate admitted-count change at its source and destination.

### Proof

Across the facet, $m$ requests move from $i$ to $j$. Applying
$A_e=\min(R_e,C_e)$ gives the displayed $d_i,d_j$ exactly, including
simultaneous exchanges that cross a capacity threshold in one batch. This
proves the stated change in $A$ and hence in $g_A$.

If a continuous scalar potential generates the constant field on each
adjacent open cell, it is affine on each cell. Continuity across their common facet
requires its gradient jump to be orthogonal to every facet-tangent
direction. Therefore the jump must be parallel to the facet normal
$P_0(e_i-e_j)$. For $E\ge3$, the jump $P_0(d_i e_i-d_j e_j)$ has the same
coordinate $(d_j-d_i)/E$ at every expert outside $\{i,j\}$, while the facet
normal has zero there. Thus a mismatch $d_i\ne d_j$ rules out any scalar
potential.

If $d_i\ne d_j$, the jump has a nonzero component on any expert coordinate
outside $\{i,j\}$, whereas the facet normal has zero there. If $d_i=d_j$,
the jump is $d_i n$ with $d_i\ge0$. When every regular facet has this latter
form, the piecewise-constant field has zero distributional curl and a
positive-semidefinite distributional derivative: each facet contributes
$d_i n n^\top$ on its oriented normal measure. Integrating on the simply
connected pressure quotient gives a convex piecewise-affine potential. This
statement concerns the field on open cells; at multiple-facet intersections,
boundary values are interpreted as subgradient selections from adjacent cells.

## Binary-expert corollary

For $E=2$, the quotient is one-dimensional, so every facet jump is parallel
to its normal. More directly, as $x=q_1-q_2$ increases, each token can only
switch from expert 1 to expert 2. Thus $A_1(x)$ is nonincreasing and
$A_2(x)$ is nondecreasing. The first coordinate of $g_A$,
$$
\frac{KT(\mu_1-\mu_2)-A_1(x)+A_2(x)}{2},
$$
is nondecreasing. It is therefore a subgradient of a one-dimensional convex
potential. The obstruction begins in quotient dimension at least two; the
theorem does not say that every $E\ge3$ instance fails, only that a single
mismatched facet is sufficient for failure.

[`admitted_feedback_noncyclicity_2026-10-17.md`](admitted_feedback_noncyclicity_2026-10-17.md),
token 3 has a regular facet at $q_2-q_3=81/100$ where it switches from expert
2 to expert 3. Just below the facet, $R^-=(0,1,2)$: expert 2 is exactly at
capacity and expert 3 is already over capacity. Hence $\alpha=1$, $\beta=0$.
Just above, $R^+=(0,0,3)$. The admitted-field jump in quotient coordinates
is $(-1/3,2/3)$, which has a nonzero component tangent to the facet.

$$

$$
All four points are strict-routing states; the minimum score margin is
$1/100$. The admitted-field cyclic sum around this rectangle is exactly

## Research boundary

The potential/cyclic-monotonicity criterion itself is classical; this
document's theorem applies that criterion to the hard Top-K count field after
per-expert clipping. Relevant MoE neighbors have different mathematical
objects. $\phi$-Balancing studies a convex potential over population routing
probabilities ([ICML 2026 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26br.html)).
The 2026 preprint [*Three Phases of Expert Routing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04230)
uses entropy-regularized mean-field congestion with a convex Rosenthal
potential and explicitly notes that its linear congestion model may miss
hard capacity thresholds. Neither source, as checked here, defines the
fixed-batch clipped field in this theorem. They do rule out broad novelty
claims about introducing potential structure to MoE routing.

This theorem explains precisely which local clipping transitions destroy
potential structure in the existing Top-K model. It does not imply closed-loop
instability, excess overflow, or a value separation from the polyhedral
adaptive-testing benchmark. The criterion is about the geometry of the
feedback field; a benchmark that reproduces the same cell labels reproduces
the same obstruction. Novelty at conference level still requires a broader
quantitative consequence or a theorem whose content cannot be transferred
unchanged to that benchmark.

## Controlled exact synthetic audit

[`scripts/run_admission_facet_ensemble.py`](../../scripts/run_admission_facet_ensemble.py)
samples 40 independently seeded $E=4,K=2,T=12$ integer-affinity plants.
Within each plant, the same candidate routing facets are evaluated under
four paired capacity profiles: no clipping, uniform exact-total capacity,
heterogeneous exact-total capacity, and aggregate shortage. Affinity entries
are iid integer uniform on $[-4,4]$; candidate pressures are exact rational
points on token-pair score-tie hyperplanes. The script retains regular facets,
deduplicates coincident same-pair facets, computes routes/counts/clipping
with rational arithmetic, checks the exact jump formula, and for every
mismatched sampled facet constructs a strict local rectangle with nonzero
exact circulation.

### Random-utility and capacitated-game boundary

The requested-load potential has a close classical analogue in random-utility
choice theory. Shi, Shum, and Song state the Williams-Daly-Zachary result:
under an absolutely continuous random-utility shock, the expected maximum-
utility surplus is convex and differentiable, and its gradient is the
choice-probability vector ([Econometrica 86(2), 2018, pp. 737–761,
 DOI: 10.3982/ECTA14115738](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA14115738)). For Top-K,
the same envelope argument applies to the expected maximum over all
K-element expert subsets: away from probability-zero ties, its gradient with
respect to the score vector is the expected Top-K indicator vector. This is
the population analogue of the requested-count potential already used in
this project; it is not a new contribution of the admitted-field theorem.

Capacity effects also have established game-theoretic neighbors. Gourvès,
Monnot, Moretti, and Thang study congestion games with capacity limits and
priority-based admission. They prove potential-game results for singleton
strategies and show that equilibrium existence can fail for general bundles
([SAGT 2012; journal version, Theory of Computing Systems 57(3), 2015,
598–616](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-014-9541-0)). Their object is a
strategic game with player costs and unilateral deviations, not the
pressure-indexed field of aggregate Top-K requests after the coordinatewise
map `A_e=min(R_e,C_e)`. The overlap means we should not claim that capacity
constraints or capacity-induced non-potentiality are new in general. The
precise result here is the facetwise necessary-and-sufficient criterion for
this particular aggregate field, plus the separately proved Top-K family
results establishing when its clipping transitions violate that criterion.

These boundaries still do not establish paper-level novelty. In particular,
coordinatewise clipping of a vector field can generically destroy Jacobian
symmetry; the contribution must rest on the exact Top-K admission criterion
and a consequential theorem, not on that generic observation alone.
