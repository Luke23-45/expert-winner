## Design and implementation

The run covers 33 cells: `E={3,4,6,8}`, with the registered deduplicated
`K={1,floor(E/2),E-1}`, and three batch sizes per `(E,K)`. Each cell uses
2,000 paired first-batch draws. Top-K subset probabilities are computed by
the exact Plackett-Luce subset recurrence with rational base weights
`(5/4,1,...,1)`; these probabilities are converted to double precision for
NumPy's multinomial sampler. World 1 is generated by the exact coordinate
swap of world 0, so the paired count laws are correctly coupled. Expected
next-batch overflow is evaluated in double precision from the binomial
marginal tail formula, not estimated from second-batch Monte Carlo outcomes.

All registered implementation checks pass:

- exact total capacity `sum C_e=K*T` and request mass `sum R_e=K*T`;
- exact world-swap construction and positive saturated/unsaturated margins;
- target inclusion solver residual at most `8.25e-13` (the predeclared limit
  was `1e-8`);
- same-world no-clipping identity `min(R_e,T)=R_e` on every draw.

The exact subset-probability dynamic program was independently checked by
enumerating ordered Plackett-Luce prefixes and aggregating them into subsets.
The two methods agreed as rational numbers for every registered `(E,K)`
pair, including `(8,1)`, `(8,4)`, and `(8,7)`.

Across all cells, the smallest saturated margin is `0.02520`, and the
smallest unsaturated margin is `0.01420`. The paired admitted vectors were
equal in `70.05%`–`100%` of draws across all batch sizes; with clipping
disabled, the paired request vectors were equal in `0%`–`10.5%` of draws.

## Registered policy results

The two registered policies classify the world using the sign of the
difference between the first two coordinates: the requested policy uses
`R_1-R_2`, and the admitted policy uses `A_1-A_2`. Ties are randomized. These
rules were registered as diagnostics. A separate exact analysis now proves
they are Bayes-optimal from the full requested and admitted count vectors
for the equal-outside-weight swapped family. They minimize expected overflow
within the two registered world-target actions in every cell because the
wrong-target action has larger expected overflow. This is a post-run
characterization and does not enlarge the action set to all pressures. See
[`topk_swapped_saturation_bayes_value_analysis_2026-10-17.md`](topk_swapped_saturation_bayes_value_analysis_2026-10-17.md).

At the largest tested batch size in each `(E,K)` cell:

- requested-classifier error ranged from `0%` to `6.825%`;
- admitted-classifier error ranged from `49.2%` to `50.0%`;
- paired admitted-vector equality ranged from `98.4%` to `100%`;
- the requested classifier's expected overflow was lower in all 11
  `(E,K)` families, by `0.00432`–`0.02233` in normalized overflow units.

At all 33 cell sizes, the requested-classifier risk was lower than the
admitted-classifier risk by more than twice the sum of their separately
computed Monte Carlo standard errors. This checks that the paired
simulation agrees with the exact count-law comparison below.

For example, at `(E,K,T)=(3,2,768)`, the request classifier had error
`0.0010` and expected overflow `0.00982`; the admitted classifier had error
`0.49975` and expected overflow `0.03215`. The world-aware target action's
expected finite-batch overflow was `0.00978`. At `(E,K,T)=(8,4,1536)`, the
corresponding request/admitted errors were `0`/`0.5`, and their expected
overflow costs were `0.01007`/`0.01746`.

## What this evidence supports—and does not

The finite experiment follows the theorem's predicted pattern over multiple
`E` and `K`: the admitted count vectors increasingly coincide under the
coupling, while the requested-count classifier becomes more accurate. It
also confirms that the per-expert clipping implementation and target-action
calculation are consistent with the protocol.

It does **not** estimate the optimal value `v_*` or prove a positive lower
bound on it; the strict result `v_*>0` is proved in the theorem. The exact
Bayes comparison optimizes only over the two world-target actions, and the
common-action value is not globally optimized by this experiment. The
results remain exactly representable as a generic censored Bayesian
experiment, so they do not establish novelty over censored-data or
active-testing theory. No hardware cost, trained-model quality, production
prevalence, or benchmark separation was measured.
